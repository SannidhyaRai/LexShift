## Reading the numbers

Skip pointers pay off when the shorter list's next document is usually more than a skip span ahead in the longer list. In this corpus that rarely happens: there are only 4,819 document numbers, so a common term's list covers most of them (df 4,217 for `evid`), its skip span of floor(sqrt(df)) entries covers about as many document numbers, and the gap to the short list's next document is almost always smaller. Over the 120 pairs only 31 skips were followed, and the checks of skip targets that were not followed cost slightly more than the skips saved. The saving is largest where one list is much shorter than the other (`anticipatori`, df 229, against the longest lists). On a collection with many more documents per term the same code would skip far more often. The engine also has a second, larger saving that does not depend on skips: when the candidates are few, it probes the longer list by binary search instead of merging (counted as `postings_read` in WorkStats).
